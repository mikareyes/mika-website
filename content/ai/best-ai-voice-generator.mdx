## How should you pick an AI voice generator?

Pick by the job, then check two things: whether the plan you need allows commercial use, and what a finished minute actually costs you. A voice that sounds great in the demo is useless if the free plan bans ads, and a tool that looks cheap per month can be expensive per minute.

Here is the cost side, using only the numbers on each vendor's pricing page. Cost per minute is the monthly price divided by the minutes of speech the plan includes, assuming you use all of it.

> **Key insight:** For a few minutes a month, price barely matters. For bulk work like 20 ad reads or a weekly podcast, cost per minute is the number to compare.

## How do you make an AI voice sound human?

Write for the ear, then direct the performance. Most "robotic" reads are really written-for-the-eye scripts read out loud. Use short sentences and contractions, and say it aloud yourself before you generate anything.

Then use emotion tags where the tool supports them. In Fish Audio I put a tag like [excited], [calm], or [confident] at the start of a sentence, and [break] for a pause. Two to four per 20-second read is plenty, and over-tagging starts to sound acted. Generate one tagged read and one untagged read and compare before you batch. ElevenLabs' newer models use a similar bracket style.

If the script itself sounds stiff, fix that first. [How to Humanize AI Text](/ai/how-to-humanize-ai-text) covers it.

## Is voice cloning legal, and how do you do it responsibly?

Cloning your own voice is fine. Cloning someone else's without permission is where the legal and platform risk starts. This is general information, not legal advice, and the rules differ by country and state.

- **Get explicit permission** from anyone whose voice you clone. ElevenLabs says this directly, and says celebrity and public-figure voices may carry copyright or personality rights.
- **Clone with real consent, in writing,** if it's a client, a guest, or a team member. Say what the voice can and can't be used for.
- **Know the platform rules.** ElevenLabs' professional cloning includes a voice verification step to confirm you have permission.
- **Don't use cloned or AI voices in unsolicited robocalls.** The FCC ruled in February 2024 that AI-generated voices count as artificial voices under the TCPA.
- **Disclose where it matters.** If viewers would assume a real person recorded it, say it's AI. That's my rule, not a legal requirement.
