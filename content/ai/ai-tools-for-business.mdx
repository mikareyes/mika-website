The best AI tools for business are the ones that remove a specific job from your week, not the longest list you can find. Below is the stack I actually run, grouped by job, with what each tool costs.

Prices and free limits verified November 2026. I re-check them every month. Some links on this page are **my personal referral links**, which give you a perk and may give me one too.

Most "best AI tools" lists are 20 to 40 tools long, written by software vendors, with no prices. The 15 picks below are what I actually run; the short section near the end covers tools I haven't used.

## How do you choose AI tools for business?

Pick by job, not by brand. Write down the five tasks that eat the most of your week, then match one tool to each. A good tool does one of three things: it drafts, it records, or it acts.

The third one is the shift that matters most right now. A chat assistant answers you. An agent, meaning an AI that can use your other tools and do the work, takes action. Connect Claude to your tools through MCP (a standard plug that lets an AI app talk to other software) and it stops being a place you ask questions and becomes a teammate that does the clicking. I explain the difference in [Chat-Based AI vs Agentic AI](/ai/chat-based-ai-vs-agentic-ai).

> **Key insight:** Buy tools for the work they remove, not the features they list. If you can't name the task a tool replaces, skip it.

## The tools, by job

### Your AI assistant and agent

**1. Claude (and Claude Code).** Claude is my main assistant and my agent. It drafts, researches, and, once connected to my other tools, does the work inside them. Claude Code is the version that works on files and websites, which is how I build and update our sites. To give Claude repeatable workflows, I install skills, which are saved instruction sets it loads for a task. Two free, open-source packs I built: [content-creation-skills](https://github.com/kingscrosslabs/content-creation-skills) for hooks, scripts, captions, and newsletters, and [aeo-seo-skills](https://github.com/kingscrosslabs/aeo-seo-skills) for SEO and AEO audits and blog posts. Free plan gives you limited usage (resets every 5 hours and weekly). Pro is $17 a month billed annually or $20 billed monthly, and includes Claude Code. Team standard seats are $20 per seat billed annually or $25 monthly, for 2 to 150 people. Set it up for your company with [How to Setup Claude for Small Business](/ai/how-to-setup-claude-for-small-business).

**2. ChatGPT.** I use it alongside Claude, mostly as a second opinion. It has a free plan and several paid tiers. Check chatgpt.com/pricing for current prices, since the tiers change often.

**3. Perplexity.** I reach for it when I want an answer with sources. It has a free tier. Check its pricing page for current paid prices.

**4. t3.chat.** A free model aggregator, meaning one chat window that lets you try many AI models. I use the free version and do not pay for it.

### Dictation

**5. Wispr Flow.** It turns speech into text in any app, including the Claude prompt box. I'm about 3x faster speaking than typing, and my prompts are better because I say more. Free has a weekly word limit. Pro is $15 a month, or $12 a month billed annually. Through [my referral link](https://wisprflow.ai/r?MIKAELA1) you get one free month of Pro.

### Meetings

**6. Fathom.** A meeting recorder that writes the summary and action items after the call. The free plan has unlimited recordings and transcriptions with instant AI summaries. Premium for individuals is $20 a month, or $16 billed annually.

**7. Granola.** I stopped paying for it and may come back. It records on your computer without a bot joining the call. Basic is free, with meeting history limited to 30 days. Business is $14 per user per month. [My referral link](https://join.granola.ai/t/vhhbzajvu7) gives two free months of Business.

### Docs, tasks, and knowledge

**8. Notion.** It holds our docs and our issue tracker, which replaced Linear for us. Free is $0. Plus is $10 per seat per month, and Business is $20, which is the tier that includes Notion AI.

### Email

**9. Resend.** For transactional email, the automatic messages your product sends like receipts and sign-in links. Free covers 3,000 emails a month with a 100-a-day cap. Pro is $20 a month for 50,000 emails.

**10. Loops.** For email marketing, meaning newsletters and campaigns. Free covers sending to your first 1,000 contacts with 4,000 sends per 30 days and a "Powered by Loops" footer. Growth starts at $49 a month.

### Forms

**11. Tally.** Free includes unlimited forms and submissions, plus payments, file uploads, and Notion and Zapier integrations. Pro is $24 a month and removes Tally branding.

### Website and code

**12. GitHub + Vercel + Neon.** This is how I ship sites without a developer: Claude Code writes the code, GitHub stores it, Vercel publishes it, and Neon is the database (Postgres). GitHub is free, with Team at $4 per user per month. Vercel Hobby is free and Pro is $20 a month; check Vercel's terms on commercial use of Hobby. Neon has a permanent free plan with 100 projects, and paid is usage-based, about $15 a month for a small database by Neon's own estimate.

### SEO and AI search

**13. OpenSEO.** For keyword research and AEO (answer engine optimization, meaning getting cited by AI assistants). It runs inside Claude, so I can ask for a keyword list or an audit in plain English. Pair it with my free [aeo-seo-skills](https://github.com/kingscrosslabs/aeo-seo-skills) pack and Claude runs the full audit-to-blog-post workflow. The trial includes $0.50 in credits, then Base is $10 a month, Pro $30, and Agency $100. I walk through the workflow in [How to Audit Your Website SEO and AEO with AI](/ai/how-to-audit-website-seo-aeo-with-ai).

### Social media

**14. Adeli.** Adeli is a social media and ads API that Claude can use through MCP. I ask Claude to publish and schedule posts to Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Facebook, LinkedIn, and X, and read analytics, instead of clicking through a scheduler. Free tier: 2 profiles, 10 posts one time. Starter is $25 a month. A comparable alternative is Buffer, which is a good scheduler if you'd rather click.

**15. LedaChat.** Instagram comment-to-DM and story-reply automations you set up from Claude. It is free and open source (MIT licensed) and the hosted free plan allows 10 live automations.

## Which tools did I try and drop?

**Profound.** An AI visibility tracker that shows whether AI assistants mention your brand. It was expensive and built for enterprise teams, so I use OpenSEO instead.

**Linear.** A good issue tracker, but redundant once Notion held our issues. One tool fewer.

**Webflow.** I used it at my previous company. Building the site directly with an AI coding agent is much better for us, and it lets me run the AEO and SEO work that feeds growth. For a small team, I'd skip the website builder.

Profound and website builders are the two I'd call overhyped for small business.

## What if you want a different kind of tool?

These are researched, not tested by me. Neutral summaries:

- **Zapier:** connects apps with if-this-then-that automations. Free is 100 tasks a month. Professional is $19.99 a month billed annually or $29.99 monthly for 750 tasks.
- **HubSpot:** free CRM for up to 2 users and 1,000 contacts. Starter paid seats start at $7 a month with annual payment.
- **Canva:** design for non-designers, with a free plan. Check canva.com/pricing for current paid prices.

## Which one should you start with?

Start with one assistant and a way to talk to it faster. Here is the order I'd add things in:

1. **Week one:** Claude (free, then Pro if you hit limits) and Wispr Flow's free tier.
2. **Week two:** a meeting recorder, Fathom free, so every call produces notes.
3. **Month one:** Notion for docs, Tally for forms.
4. **When a job hurts:** email (Resend, Loops), SEO (OpenSEO), social (Adeli or Buffer).
5. **Then connect them.** Once Claude is wired into your tools, the whole stack gets cheaper to run because the agent does the clicking. Use [How to Setup Claude for Small Business](/ai/how-to-setup-claude-for-small-business) as your first step.

If you only have one pick, choose Claude. If you want the quick time-savers instead of the full stack, see [5 AI Tools That Save Me 10+ Hours a Week](/ai/five-ai-tools-save-10-hours-weekly).

## What does the whole stack cost?

For one person, close to $20 a month: Claude Pro on monthly billing, with every other tool on a free tier. That is the honest starting number. Costs rise when you add team seats, higher email volume, or a paid tier of the SEO and social tools. Mix and match by job.
