The best AI apps for founders are not the ones with the most features. They're the few you'll open every week, and most founders need one main assistant plus two or three specialists. I keep very few apps on my phone and do my real work on a laptop, so this list is the short version of what I actually run, plus the ones worth a look and the ones I'd skip.

Prices and free limits verified November 2026. I re-check them every month. Some links below are my personal referral links, and I only use those for apps I use myself.

## How many AI apps do you actually need?

One main assistant and up to three specialists. A specialist earns a spot only when it does a job your main assistant does badly: voice typing, meeting notes, or searching with sources. Everything else is a duplicate you pay for and forget.

That's the filter I used for this list. Each pick below is a job. If you already have an app for the job, skip the entry. The one exception: if you do a lot of work in front of a screen, the desktop version of these apps is where most of the value is, so don't judge an app by its phone version alone. (If you want a full tour of what's free, my sibling guide on [free AI tools](/ai/free-ai-tools) goes wider.)

## The main assistant: pick one, maybe two

### Claude

Claude is my home base: writing, thinking through decisions, long documents, and anything that runs as an agent. Chat, web, desktop, and mobile are all included. The free plan includes web search, file creation, code execution, memory across conversations, connections to other apps, and artifacts, with usage limits that reset every 5 hours and every week. Pro is $17 a month on annual billing ($200 up front) or $20 monthly, and Max starts at $100 a month, per Anthropic's pricing page. Not sure how to start? Here's my [10-minute Claude setup](/ai/how-to-setup-claude).

### ChatGPT

ChatGPT is the easiest app to start with, and it's the one most people already have. The free plan gives unlimited text chats with its newest model subject to abuse guardrails, plus limited image creation, voice, deep research, and uploads. It has paid tiers (Go, Plus, Pro); check OpenAI's pricing page for the current dollar amounts, because they've changed more than once. I keep it alongside Claude because it's the app most people around me already know.

## Search with sources

### Perplexity

I use it when I want an answer with citations instead of a conversation. It's an answer engine: you ask, it searches the web and links the sources. There's a free tier with limited deeper searches; paid Pro is widely reported at $20 a month, but I couldn't verify that on Perplexity's own page, so check current pricing before you pay. If you care about where AI search is going, my guide on [AI search engines](/ai/ai-search-engines) compares them side by side.

## Voice typing

### Wispr Flow

I use it every day, and it's the first app I'd add after your main assistant. It turns speech into clean text in any app: Slack, email, the Claude prompt box. I clock it at about 3x faster than my keyboard. Dictation works on Mac, Windows, iPhone, and Android. The free plan has a weekly word limit, and Pro is $15 a month, or $12 a month on annual billing. Through [my Wispr Flow link](https://wisprflow.ai/r?MIKAELA1) you get one free month of Pro.

## Meeting notes

### Granola

Granola records the conversation from your computer, so there's no bot joining the call, then hands you cleaned-up notes. The free Basic plan caps your meeting history at 30 days, Business is $14 a month, and Enterprise is $35. I stopped paying for it and may come back. [My Granola link](https://join.granola.ai/t/vhhbzajvu7) includes two free months of Business.

### Fathom

I use it as the other half of my meeting setup, because it's a recorder you can add to your calls. Its free plan includes unlimited recordings and transcriptions and instant AI summaries. Premium is $20 a month, or $16 billed annually. Pick one of Granola or Fathom, not both, unless you like paying for duplicates.

## Docs and your own work

### Notion

I use it for docs, and it replaced Linear for tracking my issues. Notion has a Free plan, Plus at $10 per member a month, and Business at $20. Its AI features are limited trials on Free and Plus, and the full Notion Agent, AI meeting notes, and search sit on Business, per Notion's pricing page. Start with the free plan and decide later whether the AI is worth upgrading for.

## Free model switcher

### t3 chat

I use it as a free way to try other models without opening five apps. It's an aggregator: one chat window with many models inside. I use the free tier and don't pay for it. There's also a paid Pro plan, but I couldn't verify the price or the free limits on its own site, so check t3.chat before you pay.

## An assistant that runs errands

### Grok Bot

I use it for the "go and check this" jobs. Grok Bot is a personal assistant app where each agent gets its own cloud computer, which means it can browse and click on its own after you close your laptop. It's in early beta. Right now access is a one-week trial (card required, limited usage), Cursor Ultra at $200 a month, or SuperGrok Heavy. The trade is that it can log in for you, so start with throwaway accounts. ## Create things

### ElevenLabs

Voices and audio. The Free plan gives you 10,000 credits a month, Starter is $1 for the first month then $6, and Creator is $11 for the first month then $22, per ElevenLabs' pricing page. I've tried ElevenLabs but I don't use it a ton. Judge it by whether you make audio often enough to justify a subscription.

### Canva

The default design app for non-designers, with AI features built in. It has a free plan; check Canva's pricing page for the current Pro price, which I couldn't load to verify. I haven't covered Canva in depth, so treat this entry as a researched summary, not a recommendation from daily use.

## Is ChatGPT the best AI app?

ChatGPT is a great first app, but it's not automatically the best. It's the easiest to start with, has a free plan, and does a bit of everything. Claude is the one I'd choose if your work is writing, long documents, or agents, because that's what I do all day. The two are close, and which is best depends on which one you'll open without thinking.

If you're only going to install one thing, install whichever of the two you will actually use for a full week. If you want to compare them yourself, run the same real task through each free tier and keep the one that needs the least fixing.

## Which AI apps should you skip?

Skip anything that duplicates your main assistant, resells someone else's model, or locks you into a plan before you've used it. Here's what I'd delete first.

- **A second and third $20 assistant.** Paying for ChatGPT, Claude, and a third assistant at once gets you apps that overlap. Pay for one, use the free tiers of the others.
- **Wrapper apps.** These are apps whose AI is a thin layer over a model you could use directly. If the app can't name a job it does better than the original, delete it.
- **Subscription traps.** A free trial that needs a card, or a weekly plan that renews without warning. Set a calendar reminder the day you start, or skip it.
- **Apps with a login vault you don't need.** The more an app can do for you, the more it can do with your data. Keep approvals on before anything pays or books.
- **Anything you can't name the job for.** If you can't say what it does for you in one sentence, it goes.
