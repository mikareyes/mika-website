The free AI tools worth using are the ones with a free tier that never expires and doesn't ask for a card. Trials, credit packs, and "free for 14 days" don't count, and most lists of free AI tools mix them in.

Prices and free limits verified November 2026. I re-check them every month.

A lot of my own stack costs $0. Below is what's free, the catch on each tool, and the stack I'd start with if I were spending nothing.

## What counts as free here?

A tool counts as free if you can use it indefinitely without paying and without entering a card. That means an ongoing free tier or open-source software you can run yourself. A free trial, a one-time credit pack, or "free until your first invoice" is a different thing, and I cover those [further down](#which-free-ai-tools-are-really-trials).

Every free tier has a catch, because the company pays for the compute behind your answers. The catch is usually one of four things: usage caps, a smaller model, branding on your output, or your data being used to improve the product. I call out which one for each tool.

## Chat assistants: which free AI should you start with?

Pick one chat assistant and learn it well before adding anything else. They are all free at the basic level, so the question is which limit annoys you least.

**1. ChatGPT.** It's the one I open when I want a fast second opinion. The free plan has unlimited text chats, but with "limited" uploads, image creation, voice, and deep research. The catch is that the limits are vague, so you find them by hitting them.

**2. Claude.** It's my main assistant. The free plan includes chat, web search, file creation, memory, and up to 5 Projects. Usage is limited over a 5-hour window and a weekly window, and free is Sonnet and Haiku only, with no Opus and no Claude Code. If you're brand new, start with [How to Setup Claude](/ai/how-to-setup-claude).

**3. t3 chat.** I use the free version and don't pay for it. It's a chat app that lets you try many models in one place, which makes it a cheap way to compare them. I couldn't find a published free-tier limit, so treat it as a free way to try models, not a daily driver.

> **Key insight:** Free chat limits are set by compute, not by message count. A short question and a 50-page upload do not cost you the same.

## Research and writing: what's free?

**4. NotebookLM.** Google's tool for asking questions about your own documents. The free plan allows 100 notebooks and 50 sources per notebook. It's the safest free tool for research because it answers from what you upload.

**5. Perplexity.** I use it for quick web answers. The basic search is free. Free Pro searches are limited, but I'm not quoting a number because published figures disagree. Check the app for today's cap.

## Meetings and forms

**6. Fathom.** I use it to record and summarize calls. The free plan has unlimited recordings and transcriptions plus AI call summaries. Some AI features, like action items and Ask Fathom, are marked "limited use," so check which ones you need.

**7. Tally.** I use it for forms. Free means unlimited forms and unlimited submissions, plus payments, file uploads, and conditional logic. The catch: custom domains, custom CSS, and team collaboration are paid, and Tally branding shows on free forms.

## Automation and social media

**8. Zapier.** The best-known option. The free plan gives you 100 tasks a month and two-step Zaps only (one trigger, one action), checked every 15 minutes. It's enough for one or two simple automations and not much more.

**9. LedaChat.** Instagram comment-to-DM and story-reply automations, set up from Claude through an MCP, which is a connector that lets an AI assistant use a tool for you. The free plan allows up to 10 live automations. It's also open source (MIT), so you can [self-host it](https://github.com/kingscrosslabs/ledachat) on Vercel and Neon for free.

**10. Adeli.** One API for posting, comments, messages, analytics, and ads across social platforms, which Claude can use through an MCP. The free tier is a one-time allowance (10 posts, 100 comments, 100 messages in total), not a monthly reset, with no card required. That puts it closer to a starter pack than a forever-free plan, so I list it in the trials section below too. Compare it with Buffer or Postiz if you only need scheduling.

## Build and host a website for $0

If you build with an AI coding agent, the hosting stack can cost nothing. This is the free trio I use. New to this? Read [How to Vibe Code for Beginners](/ai/how-to-vibe-code-for-beginners) first.

**11. GitHub.** The free plan has unlimited public and private repositories and 2,000 Actions minutes a month.

**12. Vercel.** The Hobby plan is free and includes monthly usage allowances, such as 100 GB of Fast Data Transfer and 1,000,000 function invocations. It has no team collaboration features, and if you go over a limit you wait about 30 days before using that feature again.

**13. Neon.** I use it for Postgres. The free plan includes up to 100 projects, 1 GB of storage per project, and compute that scales to zero after 5 minutes of inactivity. Writes are blocked if you exceed the storage limit.

## Run AI on your own computer

**14. Ollama and LM Studio.** These are the only truly unlimited options, because the model runs on your own hardware. Ollama says running models locally is always unlimited. LM Studio says it's free at home and at work. Your data stays on your machine, but you need a computer strong enough for the model, and local models are usually weaker than the cloud ones above.

## Which free AI tools are really trials?

Several tools get called free that don't stay free. Watch for these four patterns:

- **One-time credits.** You get a pile of credits that don't refill. Adeli's free tier works this way, and so do many image and video generators.
- **Time-limited trials.** Full features for 7 to 30 days, then a paywall. Vercel's Pro trial is an example: the Hobby plan is the free one.
- **"Free" with a card.** Anything that asks for a card up front is a trial, even if it says $0 today.
- **Free with a data cost.** The tool is free because your content trains or improves the product. Claude's consumer plans, free included, may use chats for training if you allow it.

Before you sign up, check three things on the pricing page: does the allowance reset monthly, is a card required, and what happens to your data.
